How it's scored

Brier score
Mean of (probability − outcome)². 0 is perfect; always saying 50% scores 0.25.
Log score
−ln of the probability given to what happened. Confident misses cost the most.
Accuracy
Share of forecasts on the right side of 50%. Exactly 50% counts half. The market row is the market-baseline accuracy.
ECE
Expected calibration error over ten equal bins: the count-weighted gap between forecast and observed frequency. 0 is perfectly calibrated.
Sharpness
Mean distance of the forecasts from 50%, from 0 (always 50%) to 0.5 (always 0% or 100%). Read it with ECE: a model that says about 55% to everything can look calibrated while telling you nothing. Aim for low ECE and high sharpness.
Balanced accuracy
Ground truth: each forecast read as a Yes call (above 50%) or No call (below), then the mean of the share of Yes events called Yes and No events called No. Always saying No scores 50%, however many questions resolved No.
MCC
Matthews correlation between the Yes/No calls and what happened. 0 is no better than chance, 1 is every call right.
Skill vs market
1 − Brier ÷ market Brier, on the questions where both the setup and the market have a forecast. Above 0 beats the market.
Always No
A baseline that says 0% on every question. Most questions resolve No, so its accuracy is the bar to clear.
AUROC
Chance a question that resolved Yes got a higher forecast than one that resolved No. 0.5 is chance. It ignores calibration.
Abstentions
Never turned into numbers silently. Scores use answered questions; “Brier, abstain = 0.5” counts each abstention as 50%.
Market
The Polymarket or Kalshi Yes price when the forecast was made, used as a forecast. Shown in ¢.
Imported results
Aye's free-text answers are graded strictly into 0 or 1, so ECE and AUROC say little for them. Her LLM-judge scores are shown for contrast only; they aren't a forecast metric.
